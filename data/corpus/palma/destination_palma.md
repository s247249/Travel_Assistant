## Overview
Palma de Mallorca, the capital of Spain's largest Balearic island, is a compact Mediterranean city where a Gothic cathedral rises over a working marina and long sandy beaches begin minutes from the old town. Palma de Mallorca pairs genuine urban culture — La Seu, Bellver Castle, aristocratic patios, the Es Baluard museum — with an easy sea-facing life of coves, catamarans and beach clubs along the Badia de Palma. Behind the city, the UNESCO-listed Serra de Tramuntana adds hiking, cycling and the wild headland of Formentor within day-trip reach. The budget level is medium: above Spain's cheapest inland cities, below the island's premium resort enclaves.

## Vibe & who it suits
The character of Palma de Mallorca is sea, relax, family and beach. Days drift between swims and long lunches; evenings mean promenades, tapas in Santa Catalina and sunset sails. Families appreciate shallow beaches, short transfers and four-person hotel rooms; couples find romance in the old town and on the water; relax-seekers can do almost nothing, beautifully. Travellers prioritising dense museums or big-city nightlife may prefer Madrid or Barcelona; Palma de Mallorca wins when the sea sets the pace and culture is the seasoning.

## When to go
Between June and September 2026, Palma de Mallorca runs on full summer. June brings warm sea, long light and manageable crowds — the sweet spot for mixing beach and sightseeing. July heats up steadily and family traffic builds. August is the peak: hottest days, fullest beaches and hotels, and the highest prices of the year; book stays and popular activities well ahead and plan indoor or early-morning culture. September eases gracefully — the sea stays warm, crowds thin after the first week, and prices soften, making it ideal for couples and slower travellers.

## Getting around
Palma de Mallorca is refreshingly simple to navigate. The airport connects to the centre by a direct city bus in a short ride. Within the city, most sights — cathedral, old town, Santa Catalina, Es Baluard, the marina — are walkable, and the flat seafront promenade links the port to the beach districts. City buses cover the bay, Cala Major and Playa de Palma cheaply; taxis are plentiful for hilltop Bellver or late returns. Organised excursions handle the Tramuntana and Formentor, where mountain roads and seasonal access rules complicate self-driving.
