## Description
Palma Cathedral, known as La Seu, is the defining landmark of Palma de Mallorca and the anchor of any visit to the city's historic core. This Gothic cathedral rises directly above the waterfront, its honey-coloured sandstone walls and flying buttresses reflected in the Parc de la Mar lagoon below. Inside, visitors find a vast, luminous nave with some of the tallest Gothic vaults in Europe, a celebrated rose window that floods the interior with coloured light in the morning, and a controversial modern chapel intervention by Antoni Gaudí alongside works by Miquel Barceló. A visit to Palma Cathedral (La Seu) Entry in Palma de Mallorca typically takes about an hour and a half, allowing time for the cloister, the museum rooms and quiet contemplation in the side chapels.

## Who it's for
The Palma Cathedral (La Seu) Entry experience in Palma de Mallorca suits families with older children, couples and seniors who appreciate history and architecture at a gentle pace. It is largely step-free around the main nave, seating is plentiful, and the cool interior offers welcome shade. Solo travellers interested in art and religious heritage will also find plenty to absorb.

## Practical notes
Palma Cathedral is open daily and sits within easy walking distance of the old town of Palma de Mallorca, so it pairs naturally with a patios walk or a waterfront stroll. Booking entry ahead is recommended in summer, when cruise-ship mornings bring the largest crowds; early visits are calmer and catch the rose window at its best. Shoulders and knees should be covered out of respect. Entry costs are low and the value is exceptional.
