## Description
Picasso Museum Entry unlocks the collection housed in a chain of medieval merchant palaces on Carrer Montcada in Barcelona's El Born district. The museum's strength is its depth on the artist's formative years: academic studies, Blue Period canvases and the complete Las Meninas series show how Picasso's eye developed long before cubism made him famous. The setting is half the pleasure, with stone courtyards, timber ceilings and narrow staircases linking the galleries. Because the artist's ties to Barcelona ran deep, the collection reads like a hometown portrait of a genius in the making.

## Who it's for
Solo travellers can linger over the early sketches at their own pace, couples enjoy combining art with an El Born evening, and seniors will find the compact layout manageable with rest stops in the courtyards. Art students and history lovers gain the most, though the human story engages general visitors too.

## Practical notes
The museum opens daily and a visit fills about two hours. Entry is timed and the historic rooms are small, so booking ahead avoids the worst queues in high season. Large bags are not practical inside. Afterwards, the arcaded streets of El Born and the nearby Santa Maria del Mar church make a natural continuation of the walk in Barcelona.
