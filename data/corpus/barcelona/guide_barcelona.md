## Transport
Barcelona is one of Europe's easiest cities to navigate without a car. From the airport, train and metro links reach the centre straightforwardly, with buses and taxis as alternatives; allow a comfortable margin after landing. Within the city, the metro is the workhorse for crossing districts, buses cover hillside routes toward Montjuic and Park Guell, and walking is genuinely the best option inside the Gothic Quarter, El Born and the Eixample grid, where distances between sights are short. Multi-journey travel cards suit stays of several days. Taxis are easy to find for late returns from rooftop bars or flamenco venues.

## Food
Eating is central to the Barcelona experience. Look for pan con tomate, patatas bravas, croquetas, grilled seafood and Catalan-style rice dishes, with vermouth as the traditional pre-dinner ritual and cava as the local sparkling wine. Market halls make excellent casual lunch stops, while El Born and Gracia concentrate characterful tapas bars. Remember the rhythm: lunch is the main meal in the early afternoon, dinner starts late by northern European standards, and kitchens in tourist-free pockets can close between services. Booking dinner tables ahead is sensible in summer.

## Day-planning tips
Group sights by neighbourhood to avoid criss-crossing the city: pair the Gothic Quarter with El Born and the Picasso Museum, the Eixample's Gaudi houses with the Sagrada Familia, and Montjuic's cable car, castle and gardens in one outing. Reserve timed entries for the Sagrada Familia and Park Guell well in advance, and slot them into cooler morning hours. Families should alternate one major sight with park or beach time, packing a Ciutadella picnic as a reset. Young travellers can stack daytime culture with evening tapas tours or rooftops, but keep one late morning free after big nights. Leave margin for spontaneous wandering; Barcelona's best moments are often unplanned.

## Seasonality
For June through September 2026, plan around the Mediterranean summer. June offers long daylight and moderate heat; July warms up with rising crowds. August is the peak on every axis: hottest weather, busiest attractions and the priciest hotels, though local neighbourhoods quieten as residents leave. September brings softer temperatures and a still-warm sea, ideal for combining beach and culture. Throughout the window, favour morning sightseeing, midday shade or pool time, and late dinners when Barcelona's streets come alive again.
