## Description
The Catacombs of Paris Entry opens the door to the city's hidden underworld: a labyrinth of former limestone quarries beneath the southern districts, converted at the end of the eighteenth century into an ossuary when Paris's overflowing cemeteries threatened public health. The visit descends a spiral staircase to tunnels well below street level, where the carefully arranged bones of several million Parisians line the walls in sombre, strangely artistic patterns. Stone plaques mark the origins of each deposit, and passages evoke the quarrying history that literally hollowed out the ground beneath the capital. The official route takes about an hour and a half, moving from carved inscriptions and historical displays into the ossuary galleries themselves. It is history of an unusually visceral kind, and one of the most memorable contrasts to the elegance above ground in Paris.

## Who it's for
The Catacombs of Paris appeal to young travellers and solo explorers drawn to the macabre, unusual history and underground adventure. It is a strong choice for anyone who found the palaces too polished. The atmosphere is genuinely sombre, and the confined tunnels are unsuitable for claustrophobic visitors, very young children or anyone unsettled by human remains.

## Practical notes
Catacombs of Paris Entry is available daily, and booking a timed slot ahead is strongly recommended because visitor numbers underground are limited. The temperature below ground stays cool year-round, so bring a layer even during a Paris heatwave. The route includes many steps down and up with no lift, and surfaces can be uneven; sturdy shoes matter. Visitors with reduced mobility or heart conditions are advised against the descent. Cost is moderate, and the site is reached easily by metro in southern Paris.
