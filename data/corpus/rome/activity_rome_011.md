## Description

The Testaccio Market Street Food Crawl explores Rome's most proudly Roman food neighbourhood through its covered market hall and surrounding streets. Over roughly two hours you graze your way between stalls: a sandwich counter famous for slow-cooked Roman offal classics and their gentler alternatives, a pasta workshop, produce vendors stacking artichokes and puntarelle in season, and a bakery whose pizza bianca disappears warm from the oven. Testaccio was historically the city's slaughterhouse district, and its cuisine — coda alla vaccinara, tripe, rich ragùs — still tells that story. The crawl runs on Tuesdays, Thursdays and Saturdays at a medium cost level, with tastings paid stall by stall.

## Who it's for

Foodies chasing authentic working-class Roman cooking rate this above any white-tablecloth meal. Young travellers and solo grazers love the informal format, and families can please picky eaters because everyone chooses their own stall. Curious eaters willing to try the traditional fifth-quarter dishes get the fullest experience.

## Practical notes

No reservation is needed; just arrive hungry in the earlier part of the day while stalls are fully stocked. Carry small cash for individual vendors. The hall is covered, but summer heat in Rome still lingers inside, so dress light. Combine the crawl with Testaccio's riverside walks afterwards.
